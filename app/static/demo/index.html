How is this different from music recommendations in Instagram?

Instagram and Edits already offer music discovery and editing tools. Sonvane focuses on a sound-production workflow: review a scene-specific plan, generate music from it, compare scored variations, and keep the final choice yours.

Try it with a 3–30 second section of your video. Muse handles planning and conversation; TuneJury evaluates the generated music.

How the plan becomes sound

Music and effects follow different paths into the same mix.

Music

  1. Approved cuesTiming + music direction
  2. ACE-StepThree variations per section
  3. TuneJury + CLAPPreference + brief match
  4. Rank + mixYour selected soundtrack

Effects

  1. Approved effect cues
  2. Local synthesis
  3. Placed directly in the mix

Remembered comparisons adjust your personal ranking. Effects are mixed at approved times and are not scored as music.

Where does my feedback go?

“Less percussion.”

Edit the music prompt and approve the revised plan. “Lower the volume” adjusts the existing mix.

“Remember warm piano.”

With your permission, Sonvane saves a text direction for Muse to use in future matching video contexts.

“I prefer A over B.”

An explicitly remembered comparison trains Sonvane’s separate preference model and adjusts future rankings.

The published TuneJury model stays unchanged. Its A1 configuration here evaluates audio; the separate CLAP signal compares music with Muse’s written brief. These are recommendation signals, not guarantees of video fit.

02 / Make room to listen

Less guessing.
More listening.

ACE-Step makes the music. TuneJury A1 provides a predicted preference score for each track. Sonvane combines that score with music-to-brief similarity and your remembered comparisons, then mixes the recommendation into your clip.

Ask Muse to change the mix or revise a section. Save a musical direction for later, or remember an A/B comparison to train your personal ranking. Sonvane stores that memory; the published TuneJury model stays unchanged.
